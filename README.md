# swift-easing

Use this package when something must go from start to end over a span of time along a chosen curve rather than straight, such as one step of an animation. Start from `Easing<Scalar>`, a function from the time elapsed and the segment's duration to progress, and mix your own values by that progress. Make one from a cubic Bézier timing curve with `.bezier(_:)` or, as four numbers, `.bezier(_:_:_:_:)`, from a `Spring` from swift-spring with `.spring(_:velocity:)` or, written in source, `.spring(frequency:ratio:)`, or name one with `.linear`, `.hold`, `.smooth` or `.power(_:)`. Turn any easing around with `reversed` or make it the same from both ends with `symmetric`. Nothing here keeps state between calls.

## Laws

- An `Easing` is a function from elapsed time and segment duration to progress, in the units the caller measures time in. `.linear` is elapsed over duration. `.hold` is zero throughout. `init(fraction:)` makes an easing from a function of elapsed over duration. Progress is given as it comes, including below zero and above one, so whatever mixes values by it must extrapolate if its easings overshoot.
- `Easing.bezier(_:)` takes the timing curve as a cubic `Bezier<Point<2, Scalar>>` from (0, 0) to (1, 1) whose two inner control points have x in 0…1, which is what keeps x from turning back. It fails with `.degree` for another number of control points, `.endpoints` for other end points or an inner y that is not finite, and `.monotone` for an inner x outside 0…1. The progress at a fraction is the curve's y where its x equals the fraction, found by bisecting the parameter a fixed number of times with additions and multiplications only, so the same fraction gives the same bits everywhere. At a fraction of zero or less it is 0 and at one or more it is 1. Where the curve's x barely moves, y is only as accurate as that allows.
- `Easing.spring(_:velocity:)` is one minus the spring's displacement after the elapsed time, released from one with the given velocity toward the target, in progress per unit of time. It ignores the segment's duration: the spring is not stretched to reach the segment's end, and if it has not settled by then the progress jumps there. `Spring.settling(within:from:)` says how long to leave it. `spring(frequency:ratio:velocity:)` is the same easing of `Spring(frequency:ratio:)`, for numbers written in source: where that would throw, it traps.
- `Easing.bezier(_:_:_:_:)` is `bezier(_:)` of the curve through (0, 0), the two inner points and (1, 1), for numbers written in source: where `bezier(_:)` would throw, it traps. `.smooth` is 3u² − 2u³ of the fraction u, and `.power(_:)` is u to a whole power of at least one, both by multiplication alone, 0 at a fraction of zero or less and 1 at one or more. An easing's `reversed` is 1 − progress(duration − elapsed, duration), the easing a segment needs to retrace the values of one on the original from its end to its start, and reversing twice gives the original's values up to rounding. Its `symmetric` is progress(2 × elapsed, duration) / 2 over the first half of the duration and 1 − progress(2 × (duration − elapsed), duration) / 2 over the second: one half at the middle.
